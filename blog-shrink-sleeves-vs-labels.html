Shrink Sleeves vs. Pressure-Sensitive Labels: Which One Fits Your Product?

Both get your artwork onto the product. They do it very differently, and the right choice depends on your container and your goals.

When a brand moves from idea to production, one of the first printing questions is whether to use a shrink sleeve or a pressure-sensitive label. Both can look great. Here's how they differ.

What a shrink sleeve is

A shrink sleeve is a printed film tube that slides over the container and shrinks tight with heat. Because it covers the whole surface, often 360 degrees and from top to bottom, your artwork can wrap the entire product. That's why sleeves are so popular on 12 oz cans for energy drinks and shakes.

  • Best for: cans, contoured bottles, and designs that need full coverage
  • Strengths: edge-to-edge artwork, follows curves, bold shelf presence
  • Watch for: artwork has to account for how the film shrinks around curves, so text and logos are planned with distortion in mind

What a pressure-sensitive label is

A pressure-sensitive label is printed on a roll and applied with adhesive, by hand or on a labeling machine. It's the most common label on supplement tubs, bottles and jars.

  • Best for: tubs, capsule bottles, jars and pouches
  • Strengths: flexible sizes, front, back and neck labels, easy to update artwork between runs
  • Watch for: the container needs a smooth label panel; smooth-walled tubs make application cleaner

How to decide

  • Container shape: strong curves or a can favor a sleeve; a straight-walled tub favors a label.
  • Design: if your brand depends on full-wrap artwork, go sleeve. If you need a clean front panel and a dense facts panel on the back, labels work well.
  • How often artwork changes: frequent flavor or regulatory updates are usually simpler with labels.
  • Your filler: ask your co-manufacturer what their line applies.

Color matters either way

Whichever you choose, press-match your Pantone colors so labels, sleeves, tubs and lids all look like one brand. A color that drifts between the sleeve and the lid is the first thing customers notice.
