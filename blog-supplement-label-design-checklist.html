What Goes on a Supplement Label: A Design Checklist

A great supplement label has to do two jobs: meet the rules and sell the product. Use this checklist before you send artwork to print.

Supplement labels carry more required information than most consumer products, and they still need to stand out on a crowded shelf. Here is a checklist to run through before your artwork goes to print.

The required parts (US dietary supplements)

In the US, FDA labeling rules for dietary supplements generally call for:

  • Statement of identity: the product name that identifies it as a supplement, for example "Creatine Monohydrate Dietary Supplement."
  • Net quantity of contents: how much is in the container, by count or weight.
  • Supplement Facts panel: serving size, servings per container and the dietary ingredients with amounts per serving.
  • Other ingredients: listed outside the Supplement Facts panel.
  • Name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer or distributor.
  • The FDA disclaimer whenever the label makes a structure/function claim.
  • Allergen information where it applies.

Rules change and details matter, so have a regulatory specialist review your final label. This checklist is a starting point, not legal advice.

Layout tips that help it sell

  • One hero message on the front. The product name, the flavor and one reason to buy. Everything else goes to the side or back.
  • Design for the real label size. Measure the label panel on the actual tub. Our 25 oz takes a label up to 4.75 in tall.
  • Keep text legible. Tiny, low-contrast text is hard to read and easy to get wrong in print.
  • Plan the facts panel early. It is often the biggest block on the label, so leave it room from the first draft.
  • Match your colors. Specify Pantone codes so the label matches your tub and lid.

Before you send it to print

  • Check bleed, safe area and the exact label size.
  • Confirm whether it's a front, back or wraparound label.
  • Ask for a press-matched proof.
