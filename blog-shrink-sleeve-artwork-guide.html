Shrink Sleeve Artwork: The White Layer, Clear Areas and the Seam

A shrink sleeve file is not the same as a label file. These are the parts that decide whether the sleeve on the can looks like the design on your screen.

A shrink sleeve is printed flat on clear film, seamed into a tube and shrunk onto the can with heat. Artwork that looks right on screen can come out wrong if the file isn’t built for that process. These are the parts of a sleeve file that matter most.

Start from the sleeve template

Every can format has its own sleeve size. We print sleeves for 12 oz, 16 oz, slim and sleek cans, and each one gets its own template. Build the artwork on it. If your design was made for another process, such as direct digital print on the can, it has to be resized to the sleeve template before it can be proofed.

Plan the white layer

Because the film is clear, a white layer is printed behind the artwork so the colors look solid on a metal can. Everything printed on top of the white shows in full color. On your proof, the white layer is shown as its own color, so you can see exactly where it sits. Light parts of the design, like white type or cream-colored artwork, depend on that white layer to show up at all.

Mark the clear areas

Anywhere you leave the white out, the bare can shows through. That can be a deliberate effect, like a metallic logo or a window to the can. Mark those areas in the file as clear knockouts and name them plainly. A swatch called “silver” or “can” can be read as a printed silver ink or as bare can, so say which one you mean.

Keep the seam clear

Where the film overlaps to form the tube there is a seam, and a narrow strip along it is kept free of ink. Keep logos, text and barcodes away from that strip.

Protect barcodes and small text

Film shrinks most around the shoulder and the base of the can. Keep the barcode, the supplement or nutrition panel and small text on the straight body of the can, where the shape changes least. If the sleeve has a foil effect, keep the barcode out of it so it scans cleanly.

Check every flavor

Badges, icons and callouts that repeat across flavors should either match exactly or differ on purpose. When a range has several flavors, it is easy for one file to miss a badge or carry an old version. Check each flavor against the others on the proof.

Tell us how your filler runs

Sleeves arrive at your filler seamed and on rolls. The roll has to unwind the way their line expects, for example with the top of the artwork coming off first. Get that from your filler and send it with the artwork.

Before you approve the proof

  • The white layer sits behind everything that should look solid
  • Clear areas are only where you want the can to show
  • Nothing important crosses the seam strip
  • The barcode is on the straight body and outside any foil
  • Every flavor has the same badges and details
  • The wind direction matches your filler’s line
