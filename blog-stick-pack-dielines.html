Stick Pack Dielines: Single-Stick vs. Multi-Stick Files

A stick pack file can look perfect and still print with the design in the wrong place. It usually comes down to how the dieline was set up.

Stick packs are printed on a roll of film, then formed, filled, sealed and cut on a packaging machine. Your artwork has to line up with where that machine seals and cuts. When it doesn’t, a design element meant for the top of the stick can end up at the bottom.

Single-stick and multi-stick files

Stick pack artwork is often supplied as a multi-stick file: several sticks side by side, the way the film runs across the machine. It can also be supplied as a single stick that gets repeated. Problems start when one is converted into the other. If a multi-stick layout is rebuilt as a single stick, or the reverse, the artwork can shift against the seals and the cut. A color band designed for the top of each stick can land at the bottom of the next one.

Set up one master dieline

  • Keep one approved dieline per stick format, from the line that fills it.
  • Mark the top and bottom of the stick, the seal areas and the cut line on it.
  • Mark the tear notch, if your stick has one.
  • Keep text, logos and color bands that have to sit in a set place inside the area between the seals.

Check the conversion

If a file has to be rebuilt from multi-stick to single-stick, or the reverse, ask for both versions on the proof. Compare one stick from each, edge to edge, before you approve. It takes a few minutes and can save a full run.

Match the film to the machine

The film has to suit the filling machine, not only the design. A film that is too thick or the wrong structure may not seal or form properly on that line. Get the film specs from your filler before printing.

What to get from your filler

  • Their dieline or template for the stick size
  • Film width and the repeat length of each stick
  • Seal areas and the cut position
  • Which end of the stick comes off the roll first
  • Film thickness and structure their machine runs
