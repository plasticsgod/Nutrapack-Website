Soft Touch, Matte or Gloss: What Each Label Finish Does to Your Colors

The finish is a clear layer over your print, and it changes how your colors look. Here is what each one does, so the label you get matches the label you approved.

Most product labels get a lamination: a thin clear layer over the print that protects it from scuffs, moisture and handling. The three common choices are gloss, matte and soft touch. Each one feels different, and each one changes how your colors look, especially black.

Gloss

Gloss makes colors look bright and saturated and gives the deepest, richest black. It also reflects light, so it shows glare under shop lights and fingerprints in the hand. Choose gloss when color punch matters most.

Matte

Matte cuts glare and gives a calmer, premium look. It softens colors a little and adds a slight gray to deep blacks, though less than soft touch does.

Soft touch

Soft touch gives a velvety feel that people notice the moment they pick up the pack. It is a great fit for products meant to be held. It is also an extra layer over the film, a bit like a strip of tape over a printed page: when it lays down, it adds a slight gray haze. Solid blacks look less deep and can look faded or speckled up close, especially in photos. That is normal for the finish, not a print fault.

Which one for which look

  • Deep, solid black is the brand: gloss.
  • Premium and low-glare, with some black: matte.
  • Feel matters most, and the design isn’t built on solid black: soft touch.

Judge the finish in hand

A screen can’t show a finish. Before you choose, look at real printed samples in normal light, not only photos, which exaggerate the gray of soft touch. Ask for our sample kit to compare finishes side by side.

Decide before you approve the color

Because the finish changes color, decide it before you sign off on color. A black approved on a gloss sample will look different under soft touch. Approve color and finish together.
