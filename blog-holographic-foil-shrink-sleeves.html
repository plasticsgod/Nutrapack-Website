Holographic Foil Shrink Sleeves: Cracked-Ice and Shard Effects

A holographic foil turns a can into the one people pick up. Here is how foil works on a shrink sleeve and what to decide before the proof.

Holographic foil on a shrink sleeve catches light as the can turns, so it stands out on a cooler shelf in a way flat ink can’t. Foil patterns come in many looks, including cracked-ice and shard patterns, and newer versions with more color play in the rainbow. The pattern is only half the decision. The other half is how the foil sits in your artwork.

Foil as a base, or foil over the art

This is the first question to answer, and your file has to show it:

  • Foil as a base. The foil sits under the artwork and only shows through where you want it, like a logo, a stripe or a background area. Everywhere else, the white layer and the colors cover it.
  • Foil over the art. The foil effect covers the artwork, so the whole design shimmers.

If your file has a white layer covering the entire sleeve, it would hide a base foil completely. So if you want foil to show in certain places, mark exactly where in the file.

Keep the barcode out

Barcodes need solid, flat contrast to scan. Keep the foil out of the barcode area, and out of small text that has to be read.

Softer, ghosted effects

Foil doesn’t have to be all-over shine. Screened or shadowed areas can let the foil show through faintly, for a ghosted look behind a logo or a flavor name. It takes careful file setup, so plan it with us before final artwork.

Finishes that pair with foil

  • Gloss lamination keeps the foil bright and the colors deep.
  • Tactile spot effects add a raised feel to a logo or detail, so the can is noticed by hand as well as by eye.

What to put in your file

  • The foil pattern you want
  • Whether foil is a base or goes over the art
  • A separate layer showing exactly where foil shows
  • The white layer, kept off any area where the foil should show
  • Barcode and small text outside the foil

The proof shows the foil areas and the white layer, so you can check both before production.
