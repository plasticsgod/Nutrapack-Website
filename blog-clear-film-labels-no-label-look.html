Custom Label Printing on Clear Film: How to Get the No-Label Look

Clear film labels let the jar or bottle show through, so your artwork looks printed straight onto the pack. Here is how they work, where they fit and what goes wrong.

Some brands want the label to disappear. They want the logo and key text to sit on the pack, not on a white rectangle. For that, custom label printing on clear film is the usual answer.

It looks simple. It is not always simple. Clear film behaves differently from paper or white film, and a few choices at the artwork stage decide whether the result looks sharp or washed out.

What a clear film label is

It is a pressure-sensitive label printed on a transparent film, with a clear adhesive. Where there is no ink, you see the container. Where there is ink, you see your design.

That is the whole appeal. A frosted jar, a glass bottle or a coloured tub can stay visible, and your branding sits on top.

Why white ink matters

Standard colour ink is slightly see-through. On clear film, it takes on the colour of whatever is behind it. Light colours can look dull. Yellow on a dark tub can nearly vanish.

The fix is a layer of white ink under the colours. Printers call it a white underprint or a white flood. The image on this page shows a clear label with a white flood behind the brand area.

You have three common options:

  • No white. Fully see-through. Works for dark text on a light or clear pack.
  • Spot white. White only behind the logo or key graphics. The rest stays clear.
  • Full white flood. A solid white panel behind the main brand area. Colours look true, and the edge of the panel can be shaped to match the design.

Most good clear labels use spot white or a partial flood. Ask for it by name in your brief.

Where clear labels work well

  • Glass and clear PET bottles for beverages and oils
  • Frosted or clear cosmetic jars and tubes
  • Supplement jars where you want the product visible
  • Metallic or coloured containers where a white label would feel heavy
  • Products with a premium or minimal look

They also pair well with other finishes. Foil, spot gloss and holographic elements can sit on clear film. If you need brand protection too, see our security features.

Check these before you approve custom label printing

Run through this list with your artwork and your actual container.

  • Look at what is inside. Dark liquid, pale powder or bright gummies change how the label reads. Test with the real product.
  • Keep small text on solid backing. Ingredients and warnings are hard to read through a busy background. Put them on white or a solid colour.
  • Check the surface. Oily, dusty or very textured surfaces can stop the adhesive from holding. Curved or squeezable packs need the right film and adhesive.
  • Plan for air bubbles. On clear film, a trapped bubble shows. Smooth application matters, and so does your line setup. If a co-packer applies the labels, read our guide to roll labels for machine application.
  • Mind the edges. A clear label with a hard edge on a curved jar needs precise placement. Crooked is easier to see when the label is nearly invisible.
  • Match your brand colours. Colour shifts with the pack behind it. See our note on Pantone matching.

Common mistakes

Skipping the white layer. Brands see a bright design on screen and forget the film is clear. Always preview the artwork over the real container colour.

Fine detail in light colours. Thin light lines disappear. Make type heavier and darker where you can.

Required panel info on a see-through area. Supplement facts, warnings and other required text must stay readable. What is required depends on your product and market, so check with your regulatory advisor. For the design side, use our supplement label checklist.

Approving without a physical test. A screen proof cannot show how a clear label looks on glass. Ask to see a sample on your own container type before a full run.

Is clear film right for your brand?

If your pack looks good and you want it to show, yes. If your container is plain or uneven, a white film label may hide the flaws and read better. Some brands use both: clear on the hero product, white film on the rest.

If you are not sure, send a brief with your container type, the product inside and a draft of the artwork. A real person replies and can talk through film, white ink and finish options with you.
