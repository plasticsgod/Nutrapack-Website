How Customers Verify a Serial Number or QR Code on Your Product

A serialized label gives every unit its own identity. Here is how a customer uses it to check a product, and what your brand needs in place for that to work.

“Is this real?” is the question every customer of a copied brand eventually asks. A serialized label gives them a way to find out. Each label carries its own QR code and printed serial number, so one unit can be told apart from every other.

A barcode is not a serial number

The barcode on your pack identifies the product, not the unit. Every tub of the same flavor carries the same barcode, and a counterfeiter can print it too. Scanning it at checkout confirms which product it is, not whether it is genuine. A serial number is different on every single label.

How a customer checks a unit

  1. They scan the QR code with a phone camera, or type in the printed serial number if the code won’t scan.
  2. The scan opens a page your brand controls.
  3. The page checks the code against the list of codes that were issued and shows the result: genuine, or a warning to contact you.

The printed number matters. It gives customers and your support team a way to check a unit even when the code is scratched or the customer has no signal.

What raises a red flag

  • A code that was never issued
  • One code scanned many times, in many places
  • A code from one sales channel or market showing up in another

What your brand needs to set up

We print a unique code and number on every label. Your brand decides what happens when someone scans it:

  • The page a scan opens and what it shows
  • Which details sit behind each code: product, lot, date, sales channel
  • Who on your team handles reports of suspicious codes

Make it hard to copy the whole label

A counterfeiter can photograph one genuine code and print it on every fake. That is why repeated scans of one code are a warning, and why serialization works best together with features that are hard to copy, like custom holographics or micro text. To pass, a fake would need a valid unused code and the security features.

Placement

Put the code on a flat, well-lit part of the label, away from tight curves and the seam, with clear space around it. A curved tub or bottle can bend a code until a phone can’t read it.
