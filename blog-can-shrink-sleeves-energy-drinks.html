Can Shrink Sleeves for Energy Drinks: How to Plan Your Can Labels

Can shrink sleeves wrap your whole can in artwork, from the base to the shoulder. Here is how they work and how to prepare for them.

If you are launching an energy drink, a RTD coffee or a functional soda, your can is your billboard. Can shrink sleeves let you print edge-to-edge artwork on a plain can, with no need to commit to a fully printed can body.

This guide covers how sleeves work, what to decide early, and the mistakes that cause problems on the filling line.

What a can shrink sleeve is

A shrink sleeve is a tube of printed plastic film. It is slid over the can and then heated. The film tightens and follows the shape of the can, including the curves at the shoulder and the base.

The result is a full-body wrap. Art can run almost the whole height of the can, which a standard pressure-sensitive label cannot do. If you are weighing both options, read our comparison of shrink sleeves and pressure-sensitive labels.

Why brands pick can shrink sleeves for energy drink cans

  • Full coverage. Bold color, gradients and large graphics wrap around the entire can.
  • Flexibility. You can use the same plain can with different sleeves for different flavors or limited editions.
  • Special finishes. Metallic inks, matte or gloss looks, and holographic effects are possible on film.
  • Added protection. A tear-off perforation can work as a simple tamper indicator.

Prepare your energy drink can label artwork

Film shrinks as it heats, and artwork shrinks with it. A good print file accounts for that. Your printer will usually supply a template for your can size and ask for artwork built on it.

Keep these points in mind:

  • Know your can format. Standard, slim and sleek cans all have different heights and diameters. Confirm the exact can before you design.
  • Mind the seam. The sleeve has a seam where the film overlaps. Do not put logos or key text on it.
  • Allow for distortion. Artwork near the shoulder and base stretches or compresses. Keep important elements in the flat middle of the can.
  • Place the barcode carefully. It needs high contrast, a flat area and clear space around it. Test a printed sample with a scanner.
  • Check color. Brand colors can look different on film than on paper. Ask for Pantone matching, as explained in our Pantone matching guide.

Check with your filler before you order

Sleeves are applied by machine, usually after filling. The sleeve size, film type and heat settings need to work with your line. Ask your co-packer or filler:

  • Do you apply sleeves in-house, or do you need them pre-applied?
  • What is the maximum sleeve height and film thickness?
  • Does the line use steam or hot air for shrinking?
  • How do you want the sleeves supplied: rolls or flat?

Cold cans with condensation can also affect application. Raise this early so the sleeve is specified for your process.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Designing before choosing the can. A change in can size means redoing the artwork.
  • Skipping a physical sample. Screen colors and real film colors do not match. Look at a printed proof on a real can.
  • Covering the top rim or pull tab area. Keep the sleeve clear of anything that has to open or seal.
  • Forgetting required text. Caffeine statements, nutrition panels and warnings differ by market. Check with your regulatory advisor for what your label must carry. Our supplement label checklist is a good starting point for layout.
  • Ignoring recycling rules. Sleeve materials and local recycling systems vary. Ask about film options for your market.
