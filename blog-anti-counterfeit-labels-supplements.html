Anti-Counterfeit Labels for Supplement Brands

A supplement tub is easy to copy. A label with security features built in is not. Here is how to layer protection without redesigning your pack.

Successful supplement brands get copied. Fakes and refilled tubs show up on marketplaces, priced just under the real thing, and the customer who buys one blames your brand when it doesn’t work. The label is the easiest place to fight back, because every unit already has one.

Why supplements are easy targets

  • A plain tub, lid and printed label can be copied from a photo.
  • Products sell online, where shoppers can’t inspect the pack before they buy.
  • Popular SKUs sell in volume, so a copy pays off quickly.

Layer the protection

Protection works best in layers. Start with what fits the risk, and add more as it grows.

Show tampering

A tamper-evident VOID seal across the lid and tub leaves “OPENED / VOID” behind when it is peeled. It can’t be put back unnoticed, which stops refilled tubs being sold as new.

Stop easy copies

Micro text and anti-copy patterns add detail that a scanner or copier can’t reproduce. A copy made from a photo or a scan loses them, so a close look shows the difference.

Trace and authenticate

Custom holographics give a finish that is hard to fake. Serialized QR codes put a unique code on every unit, and hidden micro text can be unique to a sales channel or market, so product that leaks into the wrong channel can be traced.

Where it goes on a supplement pack

  • Across the lid and tub: a tamper strip or seal.
  • The front label: holographic elements, micro text or a serialized code, built into the label you already use.
  • Bottles: neck bands over the cap.
  • Cartons and boxes: a tamper strip across the seam and a security label.

Start with one SKU

You don’t need to change your whole range at once. Pick the product that gets copied most, or your highest-value one, and start there. Most features build into the label format you already print, so the pack keeps its look.

Plan who checks

Security features only help if someone looks. Decide what your customer service team asks for when someone reports a fake, show customers what a genuine pack looks like, and decide who follows up on suspicious reports.
