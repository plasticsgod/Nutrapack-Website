Supplement Label Printing: From Artwork to Your Co-Packer

Supplement labels go through the same steps every time. Knowing them up front saves a reprint and keeps your fill date.

A supplement label has several jobs at once. It carries the brand, fits the container, holds the Supplement Facts panel and directions, and has to run on your co-packer’s labeling line. Here is how a label job moves from artwork to the filling line.

1. Start with the container

The label size comes from the container, not the other way around. Measure the label panel on the tub or bottle: the area between the shoulder and the base, and how far around the container the label should wrap. If you already have a label that fits, its size is the fastest starting point. Brands with several products in different containers usually end up with a few label sizes, and each size is its own print setup.

2. Build the artwork for the label

Artwork should be built on a dieline for the exact label size, with a safe zone away from the edges for text. Then check the regulated content: the Supplement Facts panel, directions, warnings and barcode all need room at a size people can read. Confirm required wording with your regulatory advisor. Our supplement label checklist covers the layout.

3. Choose the film and finish

  • White film (BOPP) gives a solid, bright label.
  • Clear film (clear BOPP) gives the no-label look, with white ink behind the areas that need to stay solid. See clear film labels.
  • Gloss, matte or soft-touch lamination protects the print and changes how colors look, especially black. See soft touch, matte or gloss.
  • Security features such as holographic or tamper-evident labels, if counterfeits are a risk.

4. Match the colors

Brand colors are matched on press to your Pantone references. If the label sits on a colored tub, judge the label colors against that tub, not against a white screen. A blue label that looks right on its own can clash with a blue tub.

5. Approve a proof for every SKU

Before anything prints, you get a proof for each label. Larger label programs are grouped by label size, with one set of proofs per size. Go through every page: spelling, ingredient amounts, flavor name, barcode and colors. Approve the labels that are right and send corrections for the rest. Nothing goes into production until it is approved, and corrected labels come back as revised proofs.

6. Set up the rolls for the line

Labels for machine application ship on rolls. The core size, roll diameter and unwind direction have to match your co-packer’s labeler. Get those specs before printing. Our roll label guide lists what to ask.

7. Ship to your co-packer

We ship finished labels straight to the co-manufacturer or co-packer that fills your product, so they never pass through your office. If you fill at more than one location, labels can go to each one.

What to send for a quote

  • Your container, or the label sizes you already use
  • How many SKUs or flavors
  • The quantity for each
  • The film and finish you want, if you know
  • Where the labels ship

Artwork can come later. Send a brief and a real person replies.
